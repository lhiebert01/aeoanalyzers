The Answer Engines Are Already Describing Your Company. Here Is How to Read the Transcript.

A growing share of buyers now ask an AI engine instead of typing into a search box. They ask ChatGPT which CRM suits a ten-person sales team. They ask Perplexity whether a scheduling tool handles time zones. They ask Gemini who the credible vendors are in a category they have never bought from before. The engine answers in a paragraph, names two or three companies, and the buyer's shortlist is formed before any website has been visited.

Lindsay Hiebert · October 14, 2026 · 7-minute read

That answer is being formed right now, about your company, whether or not you have ever looked at it. Most companies have not. The ones that have were usually shown a number by someone who also sells the cure for it.

This post is about the other way to do it: measure first, independently, in a way you can check yourself, and then decide what to do about it.

1. Three questions every company should be able to answer this week

When a buyer asks the engines about you by name, do they find you, and do they describe you accurately?

When a buyer asks the category question without naming anyone, do the engines recommend you, and if not, who do they recommend instead?

Which sources did the engines actually read to form that answer?

Those three questions are the whole job. Everything else in AI search optimisation is a response to the answers. AEO Analyzers exists to answer them in a few minutes from a URL, and to keep every transcript that produced every number so that a sceptical CMO, or a sceptical founder, can click through and read the engine's own words.

2. A number is only worth what sits underneath it

Here is what I learned building this, and what I would want to know before trusting anyone's AI visibility figure.

The same question, asked of the same engine on the same day, does not always get the same answer. We measured our own run-to-run drift at 0.9 points. So we never claim that a one-point move means anything, and every result carries the number of answers it was built from.

Which engines you ask changes the number. We ran the same sites, on the same day, through two different engine panels and the results moved by 14 points. So every result we produce discloses exactly which engines produced it, and says in plain words that a different panel would produce a different number.

An answer an engine formed by searching the live web means something different from an answer it gave from memory. We separate the two and report them apart, because a company that is well described from memory and invisible to live search has a different problem from the reverse.

None of that is a criticism of anyone else's method. It is simply what we found when we measured our own measurement, and we chose to publish it. Our methodology is a public page. Our evidence is a public page. The published figures are pinned by tests so they cannot drift from what the product actually does.

3. We measured ourselves first, and published the result

Before this product measured a single customer, it measured aeoanalyzers.com. When a buyer asks the engines about us by name, they find us three times out of four and, when they do, describe us accurately every time. When a buyer asks the category question without naming anyone, we are recommended zero percent of the time.

We put that zero on our own evidence page, with the transcripts behind it. Not because it is comfortable, but because a measurement tool that only publishes flattering results about itself is asking you to take something on faith, and the entire point of this tool is that you should not have to.

4. Measure and manage. The fixing is yours.

AEO Analyzers tells you exactly what the engines read, what they said, and what would have to be different for the answer to change. It does not implement, edit or publish anything on your site, and it does not sell a retainer to do so.

That boundary is deliberate, and it is the reason the measurement can afford to be unflattering. When the diagnosis and the cure come from the same place, the diagnosis always has something to prove. When they are separated, you can hand the finding to your own team, your agency, or the consultant you already trust, and they can check it against the transcripts before they spend an hour on it. You can lead a horse to water. The drinking is up to the horse, and that is how it should be.

5. What this looks like in practice

A URL. A few minutes. Three answers, each with its engine panel disclosed, its answer count disclosed, and its transcripts one click away. Each buyer question tagged by intent, so you can see whether you were measured on the questions closest to a purchase. A ranked list of the sources the engines actually cited, so you know which pages on the web are deciding your answer. And a saved record, so that when you re-measure next month the comparison is like for like.

The engines are already describing your company. The only question is whether you have read the transcript.
