What is answer engine optimization (AEO) software?

AEO software measures whether AI answer engines — ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini — mention or recommend a business when someone asks a question, and reports what to change. It is not the same as rank tracking. A rank tracker tells you where a page sits in a list of links; an AEO tool asks a question and reads the answer.

The category is also called GEO, generative engine optimization. See the section below on the difference.

Which tools track AI search visibility?

The tools named most often when we ask AI engines this question, in our own measurement, are Profound, Otterly AI, Peec AI, SE Ranking, AirOps, Scrunch AI, AthenaHQ and Rankscale, alongside broader SEO suites that have added AI-visibility features, including Semrush, Ahrefs, Conductor and HubSpot.

How that list was produced, so you can weigh it. We asked four engines ten unbranded category questions, five times each, in our September 2026 measurement — 200 recorded answers — and counted how many answers mentioned each product at least once. Profound and Otterly AI each appeared in 119, Peec AI in 89, Semrush in 77, Ahrefs in 56, SE Ranking in 45, AirOps in 32, Scrunch AI in 31, AthenaHQ in 29 and Rankscale in 16. AEO Analyzers appeared in 0 of 200. The transcripts are stored and the question set is published.

That is a measurement of what engines say, not of product quality. A tool can be excellent and unnamed, and being named often is partly a function of how long a vendor has been publishing.

Profound vs Otterly AI: how do you choose?

We are not the right source for that comparison and we will not pretend otherwise. Both are competitors of ours, and a head-to-head written by a third competitor is worth very little. What we can offer is a method: ask each vendor for the raw answers behind a score, not just the score. If a tool reports that you appear in 34% of answers, ask which answers, from which engine, on which date, and whether you can read them.

The reason that question matters is that these numbers are not directly comparable between vendors. Different tools ask different questions, a different number of times, on different days, and an engine's answer varies between runs. A percentage without its question set, its repetition count and its date is not a measurement you can check.

What is the difference between GEO software and AEO software?

In practice the terms are used interchangeably by most vendors, and the distinction is more editorial than technical. Answer engine optimization tends to emphasise being the answer to a direct question; generative engine optimization tends to emphasise appearing inside generated content of any kind. If a vendor draws a sharp line between them, ask what they measure differently as a result.

What should an AEO tool actually report?

Four things, and they are separable. Whether an engine can retrieve you when asked by name. Whether what it says about you is accurate. Whether it recommends you to someone who did not ask for you. And which sources it cited instead. A single composite score can hide a business that is perfectly findable by name and never recommended, which is a different problem with a different fix.

Two more worth asking about: whether the tool distinguishes an answer that came from a live web search from one that came from the model's memory, and whether it stores the answer text so you can reproduce a figure or dispute it.

How can I check my own brand right now?

You do not need a tool for the first look. Open ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini, ask each the question a buyer would ask — not your brand name, the category — and write down which companies get named. Do it three times per engine, because the answers vary. That is the whole method; the tools automate it, add repetition, and keep the receipts.
