## Before you send — three rules

**1. The finding must be real.** [SWEEP FINDING] comes from that prospect's own stored
citation sweep, with the transcript behind it. Never estimate, never round up, never write a
number you have not run. If a sweep comes back thin, use the branded or entity-collision
finding instead. A single invented number ends the conversation and the credibility.

**2. Meter the sends.** About eight personalized emails a week. The warmed mailbox is the
asset; a blast burns it.

**3. Stop the sequence the moment they reply.** Then switch to the reply blocks near the end.

## Subject lines

Use one per prospect. The first two perform best because they are specific and not a pitch.

- Quick AI-visibility check on [DOMAIN]
- [COMPETITOR] is getting recommended instead of [THEIR BRAND]
- What ChatGPT says when someone asks for [THEIR BRAND]
- I scored my own tool zero and published it

Do not use: anything with "quick question", "touching base", "circling back", or an emoji.

## The fifteen-minute call

Do not demo the product. Read them their own result.

1. **Two minutes.** What they already believe about their AI visibility.
2. **Six minutes.** Their sweep, on screen. The branded number, the category number, who gets
   recommended instead, and the exact questions they lose.
3. **Four minutes.** The three layers and which one their result points at. One layer only.
4. **Three minutes.** What a fix actually involves and what it does not. Then ask whether they
   want the ongoing measurement or just the report.

The close is a re-measure, not a purchase: "Fix the one thing, and I will re-run the identical
questions in thirty days so you can see whether it moved."

## Where [SWEEP FINDING] comes from

Run the prospect's domain through the sweep, then take one honest headline from the result.
Good findings, in order of how well they land:

- **Category loss with a named winner.** "On the question *[their category question]*, ChatGPT
  recommended [COMPETITOR] in [N] of [N] runs and [THEIR BRAND] in none."
- **A branded gap.** "Three of the four engines find you by name. [ENGINE] does not — its
  search returns no result for [DOMAIN]."
- **An entity collision.** "Two engines confuse [THEIR BRAND] with [OTHER ENTITY]."
- **An authority gap.** "The sources these engines cite for your category are [SOURCE 1] and
  [SOURCE 2]. You do not appear in either."

If the sweep produces none of these cleanly, do not send that prospect an email. Replace them
from the bench.
