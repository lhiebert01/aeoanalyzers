## What a Citation Sweep actually does

A sweep runs your brand's real questions against the major AI answer engines — **ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Claude** — and captures what each one actually says. Not a simulation. The live answer, the way your customer would see it.

It runs two kinds of queries, because they measure two different things:

- **Branded queries** ("who is *your brand*") test **retrievability** — when someone asks about you *by name*, does the engine know who you are and describe you accurately, or does it draw a blank?
- **Category queries** ("best compact SUV," "top project-management tools for agencies") test the **citation win** — when someone asks about your *category* with no brand named, does the answer include you, or hand the customer to a competitor?

The gap between those two numbers is often the whole story. Plenty of brands are perfectly retrievable by name and completely absent from the category answer where the actual buying decision happens.

---

## Why we run every query more than once

Here's something most people don't realize until they watch it happen: **AI answers aren't deterministic.** Ask the same engine the same question three times and you can get three different sets of sources. Run a sweep once and you might catch a lucky citation — or miss a reliable one.

So a sweep runs each query multiple times and reports the consistency. "Cited in 3 of 3 runs" is a very different signal from "cited in 1 of 3," and you deserve to know which one you're looking at before you make a decision. A single screenshot of ChatGPT citing you is an anecdote. A sweep is evidence.

## The part that changes the conversation: *who gets cited instead*

A score can tell you that you're hard to cite. A sweep can tell you **exactly who is winning the citation you're not getting** — and *why.*

When we read the transcripts, a pattern shows up again and again: for category questions, the engines lean heavily on "best X" roundup articles — some owned by competitors, many published by third parties. The brands that get named aren't always the best product. They're the ones whose citability signals made them the *easy, safe source to quote.*

Seeing that on your own category query is clarifying in a way a number never is. It turns "improve your AEO" into a specific, finite task: *become as easy to cite as the page that's beating you.* The sweep shows you the gap; the report gives you the fixes to close it — the same paste-ready JSON-LD and targeted rewrites AEO Analyzers is built on.
