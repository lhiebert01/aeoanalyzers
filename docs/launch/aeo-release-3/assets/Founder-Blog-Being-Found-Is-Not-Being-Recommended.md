# Being Found Is Not the Same as Being Recommended

## When buyers ask AI for advice, is your business part of the answer?

**By Lindsay Hiebert, CISSP**
Founder and CEO, PIGENAI LLC

Imagine two questions. The first is, "What does your company do?" The second is, "Which payroll software should a small business choose?"

An AI assistant might answer the first one perfectly and never mention the company in the second. Those are two different business problems. One is being recognized. The other is being considered when a buyer is making a choice.

## A score is not the same as an answer

A website assessment can tell you that your pages are hard to interpret, that your descriptions are incomplete, or that structured information is missing. That is useful. It cannot tell you what an AI engine actually recommends. For that, you have to ask the engines and read their answers.

Answer engine optimization, or AEO, means making your information clear enough to be understood, and then measuring how AI systems present it. It works alongside traditional search optimization. Neither a website score nor a single chatbot response guarantees that a customer will find you.

## Three questions every business should ask

1. Can AI answer when someone asks about you by name?
2. When it does, is the description right?
3. When a buyer asks about your category without naming you, do you appear?

AEO Analyzers calls these retrievability, fidelity and Citation Win. They should be tracked separately. A business can be recognized and accurately described and still rarely appear in category recommendations.

## See the evidence before you spend

Before you pay for a listing, an article or a publicity campaign, look at the sources cited in the answers you care about. Do those sources appear in your category? What would make your own information more useful to a buyer?

A citation is a clue. It is not proof that a placement will produce future sales. The discipline that matters is to measure first and then make a deliberate decision.

## Make the work concrete

1. Start with the free AEO Score and review the gaps.
2. Run a paid Citation Sweep to get a baseline of tested answers.
3. Read the transcripts, not only the summary.
4. Choose a small set of improvements your team can actually carry out.
5. Check any suggested structured data against the real facts of your business.
6. Repeat the same questions under comparable conditions and keep the dated results.

One caution. Do not draw a firm conclusion about cause from a single before and after comparison. The engines change, and so does the wording of questions.
