### Short article to paste

# One Question Is Not a Measurement

The easiest AI visibility test in the world takes about a minute. Open ChatGPT, ask who you should buy from in your category, and see whether your company comes up. Run it a dozen times and count. It is a real result, and it is also where most AI visibility analysis stops.

A single question asked repeatedly tells you one thing with confidence: whether you win that question. It cannot tell you which questions you could win, and that is the only number that changes a plan.

Nobody buys telecommunications equipment. A hospital IT director buys indoor cellular coverage for a new wing. An operator buys small cells for a shopping mall. A logistics firm buys a private network for a warehouse with bad Wi-Fi. Each of those is a different question, drawing on different sources, with a different set of vendors already in the answer. So a zero on one broad question is not a verdict. It is the result of one framing.

Buyer questions fall into three shapes: category discovery (who are the best vendors for X), problem-first (how do I fix dead zones in a manufacturing plant) and head-to-head (affordable alternatives to a named competitor). A company can be invisible in the first, competitive in the third and absent from the second, all at the same time. That pattern is the plan.

Five properties separate a number you can plan against from a number that felt convincing at the time: several engines rather than one; real buyer questions across the three shapes, written down and reused unchanged each month; search-grounded answers kept separate from model memory; failed runs excluded rather than scored; and every transcript stored with its sources. A sixth, for companies with a common name: does the report check the engine was talking about you at all.

A telecom equipment maker I know well ran both tests in the same month. Both said zero. Only one said why. On the broad question, three of the four engines never searched; they answered from memory and named the incumbent from memory. No page on any website reaches that answer. On the narrower questions where engines did search, the sources were a short list of industry publications and analyst profiles, and the company appeared on none of them. Only one test said where the first point would come from.

Fixing and measuring are different jobs, and the measuring job has been undersupplied. A fix without a measurement is a hope. A measurement without a fix is a report.

Run the one-minute test. Then, before you spend anything on what it told you, ask twelve questions instead of one.
