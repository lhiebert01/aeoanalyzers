# Blitz packs — which one to use

Three packs now exist for the same six POSSE posts. They are not versions of one
document; they are three different channel strategies, and all three stay.

| Pack | File | Channels | Use it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | `BLITZ-DISTI-PACK-aeo-buyer-series` (.md + .docx) | LinkedIn, X | The default. Shortest copy, our voice, one block per post. Now carries hashtags and a discussion CTA. |
| **2** | `BLITZ-PACK-2-linkedin-facebook-criteria.docx` | LinkedIn, Facebook Groups | Longer LinkedIn copy built around the three-line dwell hook, plus a peer-to-peer Facebook Groups framing. |
| **3** | `BLITZ-PACK-3-linkedin-facebook-campaign.docx` | LinkedIn, Facebook Groups | Seven posts, a different publishing order (pain point first), and the most discussion-oriented framing. |

## One conflict between them, resolved

**Pack 2 offers a "link in Comment #1" variant** and cites a 40–60% reach penalty for
links in post copy. **Pack 3 removed both**, on the grounds that they are third-party
algorithm estimates rather than anything LinkedIn publishes. Pack 3 is right and that is
the position across all three packs.

Two separate things are being conflated by that advice, and they should not be:

- **Manual posts** — you *can* put a link in a comment if you choose. Nothing stops you.
  The packs all put it in the body because the copy is written to carry it naturally.
- **The autoposter** — it *cannot*. There is no comment API. Links in the body is a
  constraint of the system, not a preference, and generated copy must be written for it.

## Publishing order

Packs 1 and 2 lead with **the Buyer's Standard** — the asset, the most linkable thing.
Pack 3 leads with **the primer** — the broadest pain point.

Both are defensible. Lead with the standard if the goal is to be cited; lead with the
primer if the goal is reach. The calendar currently follows Pack 1.
