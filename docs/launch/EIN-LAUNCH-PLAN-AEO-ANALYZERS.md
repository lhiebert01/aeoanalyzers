## 1. Timing — the one decision to make first

The Lantern Post release carries a **Sep 8** dateline from the same company. Two wire
releases from PIGENAI LLC inside the same fortnight compete with each other for pickup and
read to an editor as volume rather than news.

**Recommendation: dateline Tuesday, Oct 6, 2026.** Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It gives you a second data point.** The monthly re-measure runs Oct 1. Today the series
   has two published points (Aug 1, Sep). A third makes the claim *"published every month
   since August, including the months it got worse"* — which is the actual story, and is not
   available before Oct 1.
2. **It clears the Lantern Post release** by four weeks, so neither cannibalizes the other.
3. **It lands after the outreach wave has run two to three weeks**, so the release becomes a
   credibility asset a warm prospect can look up, rather than a cold artifact nobody sought.

**If you want it sooner:** Sep 22 is the earliest defensible date. It clears Lantern Post by
two weeks and uses the Aug/Sep pair. You lose the third data point and the "even when it got
worse" line, which is the strongest sentence available to you.

**Do not** date it before Sep 15 under any circumstance.

## 2. The news — what makes this a release and not an ad

A product being available is not news. **A company publishing its own failing score is.**

The lede is the honest zero: a measurement tool measured itself, scored zero on the metric
that matters, published the number, and has re-published it every month since — including the
month the number got worse. That is a verifiable, unusual, checkable claim. The product is the
second paragraph, not the first.

**The angle in one sentence:** *AI search engines read this company's website 265 times in ten
days and recommended it to buyers zero times — so the company published the zero and built
the instrument that measures it.*

### Body — the draft

KANSAS CITY, MO, UNITED STATES, October 6, 2026 /EINPresswire.com/ -- PIGENAI LLC today
announced the general availability of AEO Analyzers (aeoanalyzers.com), an answer engine
optimization (AEO) measurement tool that reports whether AI answer engines actually recommend
a business to buyers — and backs every score with a stored, reproducible transcript.

The company launched it by publishing its own failing result.

In a measurement run recorded on August 1, 2026, AEO Analyzers asked Claude, ChatGPT,
Perplexity and Gemini twelve questions about its own category, five times each, across all
four engines — 240 recorded answers. When asked for the company by name, the engines found it
in 100% of 40 runs. When asked the questions a buyer actually asks, such as which tools
measure AI search visibility, the engines recommended the company in 0% of 200 runs.
Competitors were named instead. Over the ten days to July 31 that its crawler telemetry covers, the company's own server
logs recorded 265 visits from AI crawlers, 211 of them training-tier and 179 from GPTBot. The
engines had read the site. They had never cited it.

"Being read is not being cited," said Lindsay Hiebert, Founder of PIGENAI LLC. "Most tools in
this space give you one score and a green checkmark. That number can look healthy while the
thing you are paying for — being the answer a buyer is given — is at zero. I built the
instrument, pointed it at myself, and got a zero. The honest thing was to publish it."

The company has re-run the same measurement every month since, with the same twelve questions
and the same method, and published each result whether or not it improved. In the September
2026 re-measure, branded retrievability fell from 100% to 82% of 40 runs, driven by a single
engine that stopped surfacing the site, while category recommendation stayed at 0% of 200
runs. That decline was published alongside the rest.

The product separates three things most tools report as one number. Retrievability is whether
an engine can find a business when asked by name. Fidelity is whether what the engine says
about it is accurate. Citation win is whether the engine recommends it to a buyer who did not
ask for it by name. Each layer carries its own count, its own confidence level, and its own
fix path, and every score is backed by the stored answer text that produced it, so a customer
can reproduce any figure or dispute it.

The tool also reports what most AEO tools do not: which competitors the engines name instead,
which sources those engines actually cite in the category, whether an engine has confused the
business with a similarly named entity, and whether an answer came from a live web search or
from the model's memory. An answer given from memory is reported as unmeasured rather than
scored as a zero.

Unlike AI-visibility platforms that report a single composite score, such as Profound and
Otterly AI, AEO Analyzers reports the three layers separately and stores the transcript behind
every number. The company states plainly that a category score of zero is a starting position
and not a verdict, and it declines to build features that game answer engines rather than
measure them.

AEO Analyzers is available at aeoanalyzers.com. A free check reports the diagnosis. A Day Pass
is $24 for 24 hours of full access, and subscriptions are $49 per month for Pro and $199 per
month for Business. The monthly self-measurement and its methodology are published on the
company's blog.

## 5. Lane B — make the release an AEO asset, not just a news item

The same three levers I gave the Lantern Post session, applied here. A syndicated wire release
is a high-authority page engines retrieve; these are what decide whether it helps you win the
queries you currently lose.

**B1. Name the competitors, factually.** The draft already does this in one sentence, naming
Profound and Otterly AI, which are the two competitors seeded in your own baseline. The
sentence states a difference in method — three layers with stored transcripts versus a single
composite score — and never says they are worse. This is nominative, truthful and
non-disparaging. **Confirm EIN's competitor-naming policy before submitting.** If EIN objects,
the fallback is "platforms that report a single composite score," which loses most of the AEO
value but keeps the sentence.

**B2. The category noun appears explicitly.** "Answer engine optimization (AEO)" and "AI search
visibility" both appear in the body. Your own sweep shows buyers ask "what tools measure AI
search visibility" — that exact phrase is in the release.

**B3. Statistics and a named quote are present and real.** Princeton's GEO study found
statistics and quotations are among the strongest predictors of citation lift. The release
carries five sourced statistics and one attributed founder quote. Every one is traceable.

**B4. Verify every boilerplate claim before the wire.** The founder tenure figures in the draft
are stated as 15 years at Cisco and 8 years at Intel, consistent with the LinkedIn record of
1999 to 2014 and 2015 to 2023.

## 8. Lane E — did it work

The release is a Layer 2 move: off-site presence in the sources engines retrieve. Cause leads
effect by crawl cycles, so judge it on the **Nov 1** re-measure, not on October traffic.

- **Counts as movement:** any category cell above 0%; the wire domain or a pickup appearing in
  the authority-gap table; branded retrievability recovering on the engine that dropped.
- **Does not count:** press-release view counts, which measure the wire service and not the
  engines.
