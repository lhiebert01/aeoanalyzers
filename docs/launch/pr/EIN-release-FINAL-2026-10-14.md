## 3 · Press Release Body

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

KANSAS CITY, MO, UNITED STATES, October 14, 2026 /EINPresswire.com/ -- PIGENAI LLC today
announced the general availability of AEO Analyzers (aeoanalyzers.com), an answer engine
optimization (AEO) analyzer that reports whether AI answer engines actually recommend a
business to buyers, and backs every score with a stored, reproducible transcript.

The company launched it by publishing its own failing result.

In a measurement recorded on August 1, 2026, AEO Analyzers asked Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity
and Gemini twelve questions about its own category, five times each across all four engines,
for 240 recorded answers. Asked for the company by name, the engines found it in 100% of 40
runs. Asked the questions a buyer actually asks, such as which tools measure AI search
visibility, the engines recommended the company in 0% of 200 runs. Competitors were named
instead. Over the ten days to July 31 that its crawler telemetry covers, the company's own server logs recorded 265 visits
from AI crawlers, 211 of them training-tier and 179 from GPTBot. The engines had read the site.
They had never cited it.

Being read is not being cited. Most tools in this category return one composite score, and that
number can look healthy while the thing a business is paying for, being the answer a buyer is
given, sits at zero.

The company has re-run the identical measurement every month since and published each result
whether or not it improved. In the September 2026 re-measure, branded retrievability fell from
100% to 82% of 40 runs, driven by a single engine that stopped surfacing the site, while
category recommendation stayed at 0% of 200 runs. That decline was published alongside the rest.

[How the analyzer works](https://aeoanalyzers.com/blog/how-it-works) is a separation most tools
collapse into one figure. Retrievability is whether an engine can find a business when asked by
name. Fidelity
is whether what the engine says about it is accurate. Citation win is whether the engine
recommends it to a buyer who did not ask for it by name. Each layer carries its own count, its
own confidence level and its own fix path.

The analyzer also reports which competitors the engines name instead, which sources those
engines cite in the category, whether an engine has confused the business with a similarly named
entity, and whether an answer came from a live web search or from the model's memory. An answer
given from memory is reported as unmeasured rather than scored as a zero. Every figure is backed
by the stored answer text that produced it, so a customer can reproduce any number or dispute it.
The company's account of [why being read is not being cited](https://aeoanalyzers.com/blog/reading-isnt-citing)
sets out the distinction in full.

As of October 2026, AEO Analyzers reports these three layers separately, each with its own count
and confidence level, and stores the answer text behind every score. Buyers comparing it with
AI-visibility platforms such as Profound and Otterly AI can verify that claim by reproducing any
number the tool reports.

"I built the instrument, pointed it at myself, and got a zero. The honest thing was to publish
it, and to keep publishing it every month, including the months it gets worse," said Lindsay
Hiebert, Founder of PIGENAI LLC.

The company states plainly that a category score of zero is a starting position rather than a
verdict, and it declines to build features that game answer engines instead of measuring them.

AEO Analyzers is free to try, with the diagnosis available without an account. A Day Pass is $24
for 24 hours of full access, and subscriptions are $49 per month for Pro and $199 per month for
Business. Anyone can [run a free AI-visibility check](https://aeoanalyzers.com) today.
