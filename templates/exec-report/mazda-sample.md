## Why this matters now

When a shopper asks an AI assistant "what's the best crossover SUV?", the AI gives a short list — three to five names — and cites its sources. There is no page two. Either Mazda is in the answer, or a competitor is. This is Answer Engine Optimization (AEO): the discipline of being the answer, the way SEO was the discipline of being the top link.

We measured where Mazda stands today by asking the four major answer engines real buyer questions — each question three times per engine, with live web search on, transcripts recorded. Sixty runs total.

## What we found — the story in plain English

**1. When shoppers ask about Mazda by name, every engine knows you. Perfect score.**
All four engines correctly identified and cited mazdausa.com on every branded query — 24 for 24 (100%). Your brand retrievability is excellent. This is not the problem.

**2. When shoppers ask the questions that actually sell cars — without naming you — Mazda wins only some of the time, and it depends heavily on which AI they use.**

| Engine | Category citation win |
|---|---|
| ChatGPT | 100% |
| Gemini | 100% |
| Claude | 67% |
| Perplexity | 67% |

Ask ChatGPT or Gemini and Mazda shows up reliably. Ask Claude or Perplexity and it's roughly two times in three — and on one specific, high-value question (below), not at all. You are effectively a different brand depending on which AI the customer happens to use.

**3. The headline finding: on "best crossover SUV," Mazda is missing from a third of AI answers — and Perplexity never names it.**
- **"Best compact SUV": Mazda cited every time (12/12).** The CX-5 owns this answer — strong.
- **"Best crossover SUV": cited 8 of 12 times (67%). Perplexity: 0 of 3. Claude: 2 of 3.** This is the gap.
- "Most reliable small SUV": cited 10 of 12 (83%) — the CX-5's reliability story is landing on most engines, though Claude named it only once in three.

"Crossover" is where high-intent SUV shoppers browse — and it's the question where Mazda most often drops out of the shortlist. Every miss is a shopper handed to Toyota, Honda, Subaru, or Hyundai at the moment of highest intent — and with Perplexity, it happens on every single run.

**4. Why it's happening: the engines aren't reading your website — they're reading everyone else's, and often an old version of the story.**
Across all 60 runs, the sources the engines leaned on were third-party authorities: YouTube (35 citations), autotrader.ca (18), U.S. News Cars (17), KBB (14), iSeeCars (12), TrueCar (11), Car and Driver (11), CarGurus (9), plus Trustpilot and Wikipedia as recurring trust anchors. **mazdausa.com did not appear among the trusted sources at all.** Worse, several engines quoted **2023 model-year reviews** — outdated pricing, specs, and rankings — and even Canadian pages (autotrader.ca) for U.S. shoppers.

Your story is being told secondhand, and sometimes years out of date. When those third parties rank Mazda below a competitor — or simply describe a two-year-old model — the AI repeats it, at scale, to every shopper who asks.

**5. One thing we couldn't see — and you currently can't either.**
AI crawler activity on mazdausa.com (which engines fetch your pages, how often, for live answers vs. indexing vs. training) requires instrumentation on your side. Right now that telemetry is a blind spot. It's a one-line addition, and it turns AEO from a quarterly guess into a live dashboard — the fastest way to confirm the fixes below are actually pulling the engines back to your own site.

## What we recommend — five actions in plain English

**1. Publish current-model-year answer pages for the questions you're losing — this quarter.**
Authoritative, fast, schema-marked pages on mazdausa.com that directly answer the high-intent questions where Mazda drops out — "best crossover SUV," "Mazda CX-5 vs. RAV4 vs. CR-V," "how reliable is the CX-5" — with 2026 specs, pricing, and comparisons, written the way an AI quotes: clear claims, cited evidence, FAQ structure. The goal is to give the engines a fresh, first-party source to reach for instead of a 2023 third-party review.

**2. Fix the "best crossover SUV" gap, especially on Perplexity.**
Perplexity cited Mazda zero times out of three on crossover, leaning on its own index and community/review sources (Reddit, Trustpilot, Wikipedia, iSeeCars). Ensure Mazda's presence on those trust anchors is accurate and current, publish a strong first-party crossover answer page, and confirm your key pages are cleanly indexable by Bing (which also feeds ChatGPT's search layer). A 0-of-3 result on one engine is not a brand problem — it's a plumbing problem, and plumbing is fixable.

**3. Treat KBB, U.S. News, TrueCar, Car and Driver, and CarGurus as tier-one channels.**
The engines have already voted: these are the sources they trust for your category. Make sure every Mazda listing, spec sheet, and comparison on those platforms is accurate, current, and reflects 2026 models. An error or a stale year there is now repeated by four AIs.

**4. Publish on your own site first; syndicate with attribution (POSSE).**
Keep canonical content on mazdausa.com and syndicate outward with linkbacks — never the reverse. Hosting your story primarily on high-authority third parties hands them the equity and exposes you to platform-policy risk.

**5. Instrument, then measure monthly.**
Add AI-crawler telemetry, then re-run this sweep monthly. AEO shifts fast — the engines re-source constantly, and this run already caught them serving 2023 data. The brands that win will be the ones treating "share of AI answer" as a tracked KPI, exactly like share of search a decade ago.
